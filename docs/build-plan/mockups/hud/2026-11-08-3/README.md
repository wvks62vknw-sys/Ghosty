## The diagnosis of the room

**The room has no shot: it is drawn head-on from the doorway like a floor plan and lit from nowhere, so it reads as a diagram of a
room, not a place he is sitting in at nine at night.** Round two's evening Home (`../2026-11-08-2/home-evening.png`) has the
depth, the hour's colour and the Machine's marks, but the camera stands square in the doorway at eye height, the desk is a bar
across the bottom, the window is a rectangle behind the orb, and the "lamp" is a warm wash in the corner with no lamp in the
room. A film would never shoot a room that way. Everything else he might have felt was missing (life, a reason, his things, the
city, the time of day, the orb's place in the room) is downstream of that one thing: a room with a point of view and a light in it
has all of them.

## The three worlds

**1 · ROOM+ (`room-plus`).** The same room, the same eight things, the same marks, with a shot: the camera comes into the room,
low and three-quarter from his side of the desk, so the desk runs away at an angle, the shelf stands at the left, the calendar at
the right and the city is seen at a slant through the window. The lamp becomes a thing in the room that lights it: a warm pool on
the desk's right end and up the calendar, the brief and the board warm, the shelf falling away, the city's cool light on the left
things (the light is on the points themselves: each point now carries how warm and how cool it is lit, not a wash over the type).
The orb hangs over the desk and lights it from above, his chair is pulled out and left where he left it, his mug is by the book.
That is all that was added.

**2 · SIDEWAYS: THE WALL (`wall`).** Not a room: the Machine's own view, which is every view at once. Home is the wall of what it
is watching tonight: ten live cameras in the same scan (the desk and the book in focus, the shelf, the calendar, the brief, the
board, the notebook, the lens, the city from above, the station, the way), the orb the eye in the middle with its line of sight on
the one it is looking at, and the same callout, house and ask line under it. Every tile is a camera into the real scene (ten
viewports of one scene, each breathing), not a drawn thumbnail, so what he sees is what the phone would draw.

**3 · UPWARDS: THE WHOLE OF IT (`world`).** A bigger idea than a room: one frame from the sky to his desk. Aircraft over the city,
the city as hairline blocks lit with windows and the far towers behind, the station among them, his street with its lamps and the
houses across the way, his house with the roof lifted and the front wall gone so the room is lit inside it, the lamp on the desk
and the book lit beside it; the orb over the city, its line of sight dropping to the book. The room is still there, at the bottom,
as the deepest zoom of a world that goes all the way up: a pinch goes up it, a tap on a place goes there. The Machine draws what
matters at the size it matters: the room and the street at 1:1, the city at 1:6 behind them (its own miniature, not a map).

## Which I would build, and why

I would build THE WHOLE OF IT as The Scan's Home, with ROOM+ inside it as the room (the deepest zoom, the frame Home lands on
when he is at his desk) and the map of round two as its other altitudes (OUT is the same world landed on the street and the
city; ON THE WAY is the line). It answers his "upwards" with one frame that no phone app looks like, it is the Machine's point
of view made literal (the eye over the city, its line of sight on the one thing it is looking at), and it makes the three places
one world instead of three pictures: the film's dolly from round two becomes the descent from the sky to the desk, which is what
that film already wants to be. The room is not thrown away: everything ROOM+ found (the shot, the lamp, his chair) is what the
world lands on, so his "something along those lines" and his "upwards" are the same build. The wall is the strongest sideways
move and the most navigable of the three, but it is THE EYE's idea (W3) wearing The Scan's clothes, and as Home it loses the house
he said was good; it belongs to the SEE room and to THE EYE's own Home when that theme is built. The honest cost: the world is the
heaviest scene of the three (the room and the city together, 72,000 points and 4,200 hairlines here) and it is the only one where a
favourite is two taps from Home; both are the questions below.

## The three questions that decide it (the recommendation first in each)

1. **Which world is Home.** I recommend THE WHOLE OF IT with ROOM+ inside it. The other ways: ROOM+ alone, which is the cheapest
   (round two's build with a different camera and a lamp) and keeps round two's map for OUT and ON THE WAY, and is the right
   answer if he wants the room and nothing more; or THE WALL, the most Machine of the three and the easiest to find things in
   (every view is one tap), at the price of the house and the room he liked. Context: the second HUD is ONE INK by the
   orchestrator's call (158); THE EYE comes later, and the wall is THE EYE's idea, so picking the wall now would be building THE EYE
   first under another name.
2. **Where Home lands in the whole of it.** I recommend landing on the world frame (`world.png`) at the boot and after an absence,
   and on the room (`room-plus.png`) when he has just been in it, the camera moving between them, because the world is the frame
   that says what the Machine is and the room is where his things are. The other way is to land on the room always, with the world
   one pinch out, which keeps every favourite one tap from Home and costs the frame that makes him sit up. Context: the finding
   rule (139) is held either way; the difference is whether Lens, Gym and Courses are one tap or two on a cold open.
3. **What becomes of the wall.** I recommend it becomes the SEE room's own Home in The Scan (the wall of every view the Machine
   keeps: the room, the shelf, the city, the station, the Lens, one tap each) and THE EYE's Home when that theme is built; the
   rendering is done (ten cameras into one canvas) and the tiles are the real scan. The other way is to drop it. Context: THE EYE
   is "attention is the material" (W3) and its Home was a wall of feeds in grain; this wall is the same idea drawn in The Scan's
   language, which is what 155 asked a theme to be.

## What is in the frames, and how they were made

- **The light is on the points.** Round two tinted the whole picture by the hour; here every point carries how warm (the lamp,
  the street lamps, the windows across the way) and how cool (the city through the window) its light is (`aWarm`, `aCool`), so the
  evening is in the room and not over the type. The lamp is a point light with a downward cone (`light.lamps`), in the tokens'
  amber; the lamp itself is a base, a stem, a cone of points and one bright bulb, see-through as a scan is.
- **One scene, many cameras.** The wall's ten tiles are the same scene (the room, and the city the Machine keeps of a place that is
  not a room, at an offset) drawn through ten cameras into one canvas (scissored viewports), each tile's HTML frame placed by
  `data-tile`; the eye's light sits where the orb hangs in the room, so the things are lit as they are at Home. A point drawn
  close up sits a hair nearer the camera than its face (`uBias`, 4 mm) so its own occluder never swallows it.
- **The world is the Machine's miniature.** His house (hairlines: it has only been inside one room), the street (the kerbs, the
  lamps as warm points on poles, the houses across the way with a few lit windows), the city (hairline blocks on a street grid at
  1:6, each facade a few lit windows, the blocks' edges fading with distance, the towers round the station tallest), the far
  skyline of round two, three aircraft with their trails. The room has no ceiling and no front wall, so it is seen into from above
  and behind, and the street shows through the window wall's edges.
- **The marks.** The same `.g-*` rules as rounds one and two (the stamp, the where-line, the tag, the reticle by data-box, the
  truth line, the callout and its leader, the house, the ask line); the line of sight from the orb's rim to the thing it looks at.
  Every frame passes the renderer's floor check (nothing he can press under 44 × 44, no type under 12 px, nothing wider than the
  screen).
- **Made with:** `python3 -I -B docs/build-plan/mockups/hud/2026-11-08-3/build.py` writes the three pages and the sheet (round
  two's `build.py` is imported for the facts, the marks and the room; round one's through it); `node .../render.mjs stills`
  photographs the three Homes at 2× in headless Chromium with software WebGL (a tiny static server serves the worktree so a page
  loads the tokens, the vendored three.js and the fonts as they are); `node .../render.mjs contact` writes the sheet. Each page
  plays itself in a browser (the camera's breath, the points breathing, a point re-read now and then, the orb idle; on the wall the
  breath is in every camera); `?still=ms` freezes one moment. A still takes two seconds.

## What could not be done, and why

- **No video this round**, as the brief says; the pages are live, and `render.mjs` keeps round two's still path only.
- **The stand-in fonts** (Inter for SF Pro, DejaVu Sans Mono for SF Mono): the mono is a little wider than the phone's, so the
  stamp wraps to two lines here as it does on the phone.
- **The world's room is small on a phone** (about 190 pt wide): the book's box is held to 44 pt, the shelf's to its own size; Lens,
  Gym and Courses are not marked in that frame (they would stack) and are one pinch away. That is a design fact of the world, said
  in question 2, not a rendering limit.
- **The wall's calendar and shelf are dot grids close up** (a scan at 1.6 cm seen from 80 cm is 3 px between points in a 110 pt
  tile): they read as the calendar and the shelf by their shape and their lines; the real thing would draw a tile from the thing's
  own instrument where one exists (as THE EYE's feeds do), which is a build decision for the SEE room.
- **A test with a stranger** (STANDARD 1): the five-second claims are design arguments until he has it in his hand.
